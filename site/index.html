Four takeaways
New to betting? Six terms in plain words
Point spread
The number of points the favorite is expected to win by. "Chiefs −3" means the Chiefs are favored by 3.
Covering the spread
A favorite covers by winning by more than the spread. An underdog covers by losing by less than the spread, or by winning.
Total (over/under)
The expected combined score of both teams. You bet on the real score going over or under that number.
Push
The result lands exactly on the number, so the bet is refunded. Pushes are left out of every percentage here.
Break-even (52.4%)
Because sportsbooks charge a fee, a bet has to win about 52.4% of the time just to break even. 50% loses money.
W-L-P
A record shown as wins, losses and pushes, for example 64-44-3.
How each metric is calculated
Cover % (ATS)
Covers ÷ (covers + non-covers). Pushes are left out of the percentage and shown separately in the record (W-L-P). 52.4% is roughly the break-even rate at standard −110 odds.
Over %
Overs ÷ (overs + unders). Pushes (final total exactly equal to the line) are left out and shown in the record.
Favorite / Underdog
The side favored by the closing point spread. If the spread is 0 (pick'em), the team with the shorter moneyline is the favorite.
Spread & totals
One consensus closing line per game from the nflverse dataset. No opening lines are available.
Margin of victory
Absolute point difference between the two teams. Ties count as 0.
Home-field advantage
Average home score minus away score. Neutral-site games (international games, Super Bowls) are excluded from all home/away stats.
Indoor / Outdoor
Indoor = dome or retractable roof closed. Outdoor = open-air stadium or retractable roof open.
Prime time
Kickoff at 7:00 PM ET or later (Thursday, Sunday and Monday night games, plus late Saturday/holiday games).
Divisional game
Both teams are in the same division (current 8-division alignment).
Points per game
Home score + away score, averaged over games (points per game). All completed games included.
Benchmarks
Cover % and Over % are compared with the 52.4% break-even rate. When a filter narrows the games, each metric is also compared with the league baseline (all teams, all seasons, same regular-season/playoff setting). Points and margin are compared with the previous season in view.
Chance test
Each cover % and over % is tested against 50%, and each headline comparison (indoor vs. outdoor, divisional vs. not, early vs. late season, small vs. large spreads) is tested for a difference, using a two-sided normal approximation at the 95% level. "Could be chance" means the data cannot rule out luck; it does not prove there is no effect. Testing many groups at once will flag a few by luck.
Limitations
Closing lines only (no opening lines or line movement); one consensus line, not a specific sportsbook; standard −110 pricing assumed for break-even; the current season is partial; small samples (single seasons, playoffs, one team in one role) are noisy and are labelled "could be chance". Past results describe what happened and do not predict future games.
Team / Division filters
Keep only games involving that team (or any team in that division). In team-by-team tables they instead choose which teams appear and highlight the selected team.

How to read this

  • 52.4% is the break-even win rate at standard −110 odds. A side has to win more often than that to make money. Rates are tagged above break-even, below 47.6% (the other side is above break-even) or coin flip.
  • Is it more than luck? could be chance means the record is close enough to 50-50 that luck alone could produce it; a 7-3 record is not a trend. more than chance means it is unlikely to be luck (95% confidence).
  • The line already knows. Sportsbooks set lines using this same history, which is why most rates sit near 50%. Past results describe what happened; they do not predict the next game.
